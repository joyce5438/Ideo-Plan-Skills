# Examples

## Broad food topic
User: I want a long article about Korean fried chicken. Explore first.
Research credible Korean-language writers and reporting, then present different evidence-backed questions, provisional outlines and links. Do not invent facts or assume region/history/modernization is a required sequence. Ask which angle to pursue.

## Technology
User: How could AI change software development? Discuss first.
Explore relevant technical and user-experience sources. Differentiate developer workflows, reliability and economics only when evidence supports them; flag speculation and invite the user to choose.

## A clear scope
User: Research Japanese food's impact on Korean home cooking, focusing on postwar cookbooks, and discuss before drafting.
Research that scope with relevant language sources, present findings and promising newly discovered questions, and do not draft or require the user to choose an unrelated angle.

## Short social text
User: Write a short caption about enjoying coffee.
Respond within the requested scope; do not require a research plan or fabricate a source.
